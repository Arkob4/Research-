- Estimates differ by definition: USDA puts 2025 retail at **US$50–83bn** [S4]. We use the range, not one point

**Visual:** bar chart, China bakery market 2021 → 2024 → 2029F (RMB bn: 475 / 611 / 860), with a small inset bar comparing per-capita spend China vs Japan.

**Speaker notes:**

To be honest, sources disagree on the exact size, and USDA gives a wide range, so we show the range. The market is also very fragmented, with over three hundred thousand outlets. We aren't trying to win China. We need one store in Shanghai to work."

## Slide 6: Crumbl shows that rotation alone wears off; we are built around that

- Crumbl average unit volume: **US$1.84M (2022) → $1.16M (2023) → $1.35M (2024) → $1.14M (2025)**, down 16% in 2025 [S1, S2]
- 2026 press reports store closures and **traffic down ~32% YoY in August** (Placer.ai, secondary reporting; needs verification) [S2b]
- **Cause:** novelty-led demand fades. The weekly drop creates one visit, not a habit
- **Our fixes:**
  - **Customisation = personal occasion** (birthday, apology, 520), so demand doesn't depend on the novelty of the week
  - **Gifting** = calendar-driven demand peaks (4 festivals)
  - **Co-creation:** the best customer builds become next week's drop ("designed by @user")
  - **Membership data** lets us see when a customer is drifting away and re-target them
- **Our base case already assumes in-store demand decays to 85% of opening levels** [A5]

**Visual:** line chart of Crumbl AUV 2022–2025 (US$M) with the 2025 drop highlighted; to the right, three "shields" (Personal · Gifting · Co-creation).

**Speaker notes:**
"Now the elephant in the room. Crumbl, the brand that inspired us, saw average sales per store fall from 1.84 million dollars in 2022 to 1.14 in 2025, and 2026 reports are worse. The lesson: if your only reason to visit is 'new flavour this week', people get tired. So we don't sell novelty alone. A customised cookie is tied to a personal occasion, gifting ties us to the calendar, and customers co-create the menu. And to be safe, our base case already assumes sales fall fifteen percent after the opening hype."

## Slide 7: China's rules shape how we enter: WFOE, licence, data, cash out

*(Link to Project #1: political, economic and legal environment)*

- **Entry mode: WFOE** (wholly foreign-owned LLC) under the Foreign Investment Law. Food service is not on the negative list (needs verification against the current edition). Full control of the brand and recipes
- **Capital:** Company Law 2024 requires subscribed capital to be **paid in within 5 years**. We subscribe **RMB 1.9M**, matching the ask
- **Licences:** business licence → **食品经营许可证 (food business licence)** from Xuhui district SAMR after a site inspection; staff health certificates; SAMR food-safety and labelling rules (GB 7718 for the packaged gift boxes)
- **Digital:** enterprise-verified **WeChat Mini-Program + ICP filing** (mandatory for Mini-Programs since 2023), WeChat Pay merchant account; **PIPL** compliance for member data
- **Tax and repatriating profit:** small low-profit enterprise **CIT 5% effective** (through 2027) [S16]; before paying dividends: audit, 10% statutory reserve, tax clearance; dividend WHT **10%** (treaty rate may be 5%, depending on the parent's jurisdiction) [S16, S17]. Sanctions-related bank friction for a Russian parent is a risk (needs verification)

**Visual:** horizontal timeline Nov 2026 → Mar 2027: WFOE registration (4–8 wks) → lease & fit-out → SAMR inspection → food licence → Mini-Program ICP filing → soft opening. Under it, a "cash-out pipeline": Profit → CIT 5% → 10% reserve → audit → dividend → WHT → parent.

**Speaker notes:**
"This is where our first project comes in. We enter as a wholly foreign-owned enterprise: it costs more to set up than a joint venture, but we keep control of recipes and brand. Under the 2024 Company Law we have five years to pay in our capital. Before opening we need the food business licence from the district market regulator, and our Mini-Program needs an ICP filing. On tax we'd qualify as a small low-profit enterprise at five percent. Getting money out is possible but slow: audit, reserve, tax clearance, then withholding tax."

## Slide 9: Dough made centrally, baked to order, hot at the door in 30 minutes

- **Central kitchen (shared, licensed unit, ~20 min away):** mixes and portions dough balls daily; chilled at 0–4 °C; **72h shelf life** [A7]
- **06:30 cold-chain shuttle** → store; **bake-off in store** in ~12 min per tray, toppings and inscriptions added by hand
- **Last mile:** Meituan + Ele.me riders, **3 km / ≤30 min**, insulated packaging; pickup orders via Mini-Program
- **Gift boxes:** baked the same day, sealed, **SF Express** next-day across Shanghai and the Yangtze River Delta (shelf-life test needed) [A8]
- **Inventory control:** Mini-Program pre-orders + 4-week sales history → next-day dough plan; **waste target ≤5%**

**Visual:** left-to-right flow diagram: Local suppliers → Central kitchen → Cold shuttle → Store oven → (a) counter (b) Meituan/Ele.me rider (c) SF Express gift. Time and temperature labels on each arrow.

**Speaker notes:**
"Operations follow a hub-and-spoke model. Dough is mixed and portioned in a shared licensed kitchen, so we don't build our own factory for one store. A van brings chilled dough every morning. The store only bakes and decorates, which keeps the shop small and the cookies hot. Delivery goes through Meituan and Ele.me within three kilometres, and gift boxes go out by SF Express. Because customers order in our app, we know tomorrow's demand quite well and can keep waste under five percent."

## Slide 10: Seed the content, own the customer, rent the platforms

- **Pre-launch (Jan–Feb 2027):** seed **100 KOCs** (micro-creators) on **Xiaohongshu** with free custom cookies; Douyin teaser "design the opening flavour" vote

- **Own the customer:** Mini-Program **membership** (stamp card, birthday cookie, early access to drops), so more orders run through our app and fewer through the platforms
- **Calendar peaks:** 520 · 七夕 · 中秋 · 春节 gift campaigns, plus corporate gifting (logo cookies) for Jing'an offices
- Budget: **RMB 150k launch + RMB 360k/year ongoing** (~7% of base revenue)

**Visual:** funnel: Xiaohongshu/Douyin (awareness) → store/打卡 (trial) → Mini-Program (membership) → repeat and gifting. A 12-month calendar strip with the four festival peaks marked.

**Speaker notes:**
"Marketing in China is content-first. Before we open, we send free custom cookies to a hundred small creators on Xiaohongshu, because micro-creators are cheaper and more trusted than big influencers. We let Douyin users vote on the opening flavour. Then everything pushes people into our Mini-Program: free inscription for the first thousand members, birthday cookies, early access to drops. Delivery platforms are great for reach, but they take about twenty percent. So we rent their traffic and try to own the relationship."

## Slide 11: Each cookie earns RMB 16 before fixed costs

**Per order (base case, net of VAT):**

| | In-store / pickup | Delivery (Meituan/Ele.me) | Gift box (SF) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cookies per order | 2 | 3 | 6 |
| Net revenue | **RMB 60** ($8.96) | **RMB 82** ($12.24) (list 90 − 8 promo) | **RMB 168** ($25.07) |
| Ingredients (RMB 8/cookie) | −16 | −24 | −48 |
| Packaging | −4 | −6 −2 (insulated bag) | −15 (tin) |
| Platform commission 20% / payment fee | −0.4 | −16.4 | −1.0 |
| SF Express | – | – | −18 |
| **Contribution** | **39.6 (66%)** | **33.6 (41%)** | **86.0 (51%)** |

**Per cookie (year-1 blended, base):** net price **RMB 28.77 ($4.29)** → variable cost 12.79 → **contribution 15.98 (56%)** → fixed cost 12.76 → **EBITDA RMB 3.22 (11%)**

- For reference, a Crumbl single sells at **US$4.49–4.99** [S5]; our price is on par
- **Break-even: ~165 orders/day**; base plan averages **~197 orders/day**

**Visual:** waterfall chart for one cookie: 28.77 → −8.0 ingredients → −2.1 packaging → −2.7 channel → 15.98 contribution → −12.76 fixed → 3.22 EBITDA. Channel table beside it.

**Speaker notes:**
"Unit economics. A two-cookie order in the store brings sixty yuan and keeps two-thirds after ingredients and packaging. Delivery is less attractive: after the promo and a twenty percent commission we keep about forty percent. That's why we push pickup through our own app. Averaged over the year, each cookie sells for about twenty-nine yuan, about four dollars thirty, and contributes sixteen yuan before rent and staff. We break even at around a hundred and sixty-five orders a day, and our plan assumes about two hundred."

## Slide 14: Our five biggest risks, each with a trigger and a response

| Risk | Early warning (trigger) | Response |
|---|---|---|
| **Copycats** (local brands copy within months) | Similar menus appear on Dianping | Speed: weekly drops; **brand + 甘点 trademark filed before launch**; member base and co-created flavours are hard to copy |
| **Trend fades** (the Crumbl pattern) | 30-day repeat rate < 30%; orders < 165/day for 2 months | Push gifting and corporate orders; refresh the builder; cut costs (−RMB 30k/month) |
| **Rent / location** | Lease renewal shock, or footfall drops | 3-year lease with a cap on increases; delivery is 30% of orders and doesn't depend on footfall |
| **Regulation** (food safety, licence, data) | Inspection findings | Licensed shared kitchen; HACCP-style SOPs; PIPL-compliant consent in the Mini-Program |
| **Platform dependence** | Commission rises above 23%, or delivery exceeds 40% of orders | Shift orders to the Mini-Program (member pricing); dual-platform listing |

**Visual:** risk heat map (likelihood × impact), with the 5 risks plotted and arrows showing the position after mitigation.

**Speaker notes:**
"Five risks we take seriously. Copycats: in China it'll happen, so we protect the brand, file the Chinese trademark before launch, and keep moving faster. Trend fatigue: we watch the repeat rate monthly and have pre-agreed cost cuts. Rent: a capped three-year lease, and delivery doesn't depend on footfall. Regulation: we start in a licensed kitchen with documented procedures. Platforms: they can raise commissions any time, so every month we try to move more customers into our own app."
